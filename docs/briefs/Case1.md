Problem: A farmer has 64 beds to plant a mix of tomatoes, mesclun and carrots. He has a few weeks to plan and commit to an entire season with limited manpower and inventory.

- Season length: 36 weeks
- Fixed costs: $20,000
- Total beds: 64 (16 beds × 4 plots)
- Farmer's pay: $50,000 for the season, half her time in the field → 720 field hours, implied $34.72/hr
- Temporary workers: up to 4, each paid $25,000 for the season, each capped at 1,440 hours → $17.36/hr

Hypothesis

I hypothesize that no more than 10 of the 60 beds will be allocated to tomatoes, the remaining 50 beds will be split between carrots and mesclun. Carrots 20 beds and Mesclun 30 beds can be planted to take the remaining 50 beds. Under perfect competition, the farmer will add the beds of each crop until the market price will equal the cost of producing the next bed.
