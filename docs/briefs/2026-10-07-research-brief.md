# Research Brief — Mahi Pono & East Maui Water

**Author:** Cori Mackie
**Course:** BUS 620 — Individual Research Paper
**Written:** 2026-10-07

## The challenge

A privately owned company Mahi Pono owns the EMI infrastructure which water is diverted from.  Mahi Pono relies on this water. The system was developed originally for what used to be a large part of Maui's economy, but plantation life is no longer and Maui's economic, environmental and community needs have changed. Water is becoming a significantly scarce resource, yet the system used to price and allocate it doesn't seem to keep up with changes. 

## The question

Does the price paid for access to East Maui water reflect the economic value that water creates?

## What I first thought

Initially, I thought that I would find a big disparity in price and would look at Mahi Pono as the prime beneficiary of the system and in control of water flow. 

## Hypothesis

My hypothesis is that the price paid for East Maui water does not fully reflect its economic value. I will examine what Mahi Pono pays for access, the agricultural value that water supports, and what Maui gives up when that water is diverted, using scarcity, supply and demand, opportunity cost, value capture, and market structure.

## Planned analysis

Scarcity, supply and demand, opportunity cost, market structure and value capture.

## How I would know I was wrong

I would know I was wrong if the price that Mahi Pono paid was the same as with the farmers and the community pay for the resources.
