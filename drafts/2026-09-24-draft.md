**1. The acquisition**

The key facts come from A&B's own SEC filings. On December 17, 2018, A&B signed a purchase and sale agreement with Mahi Pono Holdings, selling about 41,000 acres of Maui agricultural land plus two related companies for roughly \$261.6 million in cash, subject to contingencies and reserves of about \$19.5 million. The sale closed December 20, 2018, except for about 800 acres delivered in February 2019. The \$262 million figure you'll see in the news is this number rounded. Mahi Pono is a farming venture between Pomona Farming, a California agricultural group, and PSP Investments, one of Canada's largest pension investment managers. A&B had grown sugar on these lands for nearly 150 years and stopped sugar operations in 2016. [Alexander & Baldwin, Inc. - Form 10-K - FY2018 (Annual Financial Statement Tables - full year 2018) +2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1545654/000154565419000012/Financial_Report.xlsx)

The EMI ownership changed in stages. A&B agreed to sell 50% of East Maui Irrigation, which includes about 15,000 acres of land, by February 1, 2019, for about \$2.7 million. In 2025, A&B transferred full ownership of EMI to Mahi Pono. Under that deal, A&B is to pay Mahi Pono \$55.3 million in installments over four years, starting with \$10 million up front. sec[Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2025/06/24/end-of-an-era-alexander-baldwin-transfers-full-ownership-of-east-maui-irrigation-to-mahi-pono/)

**2. Why EMI water matters to production**

EMI started with the privately built Old Hāmākua Ditch (1876–1878, \$80,000) and grew into one of the largest irrigation systems in the U.S., diverting East Maui stream water to sugarcane in Central and Upcountry Maui for decades. It still serves two groups: besides Mahi Pono's Central Maui farms, EMI supplies water to about 35,000 Upcountry residents. [Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2025/06/24/end-of-an-era-alexander-baldwin-transfers-full-ownership-of-east-maui-irrigation-to-mahi-pono/)[Civil Beat](https://www.civilbeat.org/2025/11/the-fight-over-the-future-of-east-maui-water-returns-to-the-state-land-board/)

For current use, through the first four months of 2025, an average of 31 million gallons per day went to Mahi Pono's farming, 2.66 mgd to the county's domestic system, and 0.49 mgd to the Kula Ag Park. Mahi Pono was growing citrus, coffee, macadamia nuts, sweet potatoes, avocados and other crops on 12,244 acres as of its Q1 2025 report. For scale, EMI's own filing says Mahi Pono is transitioning 30,000 acres, 22,254 of which are designated important agricultural lands. [Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2025/06/24/end-of-an-era-alexander-baldwin-transfers-full-ownership-of-east-maui-irrigation-to-mahi-pono/)[Department of Land and Natural Resources](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/D-9T-12-12.pdf)

**3. Water as a public resource, not owned**

The clearest recent evidence is the court rulings. In September 2025, the Hawaiʻi Supreme Court ruled that BLNR violated water advocates' constitutional rights in 2021 when it renewed A&B's revocable permit. Then in July 2026, Environmental Court Judge Lisa Cataldo cut the permitted diversion from 35.22 to 31.5 million gallons per day, finding that the board violated the public trust doctrine when it approved the permit. Even the BLNR chair has framed it this way, saying the board has a public trust duty to make sure the Maui County Water Department keeps getting EMI water for Upcountry drinking water and fire suppression. [The Fight Over The Future Of East Maui Water Returns To The State Land Board - Honolulu Civil Beat +2](https://www.civilbeat.org/2025/11/the-fight-over-the-future-of-east-maui-water-returns-to-the-state-land-board/)

For the legal foundation, you'll want to cite the Hawaiʻi State Constitution, Article XI, Sections 1 and 7, and the *Waiāhole Ditch* case (Hawaiʻi Supreme Court, 2000), which established water as a public trust resource. I didn't pull those from a search, so look them up directly before citing.

**4. The permit arrangement and what Mahi Pono pays**

The last long-term water licenses expired in 1986 and were converted to revocable permits renewed annually. A request for a long-term lease was made in 2001. Mahi Pono has operated as a tenant under one-year revocable permits for seven years. sec[Civil Beat](https://civilbeat.org/2026/06/public-or-private-the-future-of-east-mauis-water-is-at-stake)

For the 2025 permit (the most recent one where I found a published rent), monthly rent was \$23,598, or \$283,176 a year, plus \$139,463 a year in funds or in-kind services for watershed management. Water was allocated per planted acre: the maximum diversion was 3,263 gallons per acre per day times the total planted acreage, so the cap increases as more land is planted. [Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2024/12/16/revocable-permit-granted-with-amendments-for-use-of-east-maui-stream-water/)[Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2024/12/12/east-maui-water-permits-set-for-action-by-board-of-land-and-natural-resources/)

The total cost is broader than the rent. Mahi Pono pays about \$3 million in total for the permit plus EMI ditch maintenance, which Civil Beat estimates at about 26 cents per 1,000 gallons, roughly half of what most farmers pay. Note that this is a journalist's estimate, so cite it as such. [Civil Beat](https://civilbeat.org/2026/06/public-or-private-the-future-of-east-mauis-water-is-at-stake)

For the current situation: in December 2025, BLNR approved a permit allowing up to 35.22 mgd through 2026, which the court later reduced. On August 14, 2026, BLNR blocked a proposed path to a 30-year license and authorized a court-ordered contested case hearing on the diversion permits. That proposal would have licensed 87.95 mgd to Mahi Pono and 6.5 mgd to Maui County. [BLNR nixes long-term East Maui water license for Mahi Pono +2](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/blnr-nixes-long-term-east-160600087.html)

**5. Data for the price vs. value tension**

This part is your analysis, but here are the two numbers you'll likely want to compare.

The price side: critics note Mahi Pono pays about \$283,000 a year for more than 9 billion gallons diverted from state land. [Maui News](https://www.mauinews.com/news/local-news/2025/07/east-maui-residents-want-more-transparency-with-ag-giants-water-usage/)

The value side, which is the strongest data point you have: the sale contract itself put a price on the water. A&B deferred about \$62 million of revenue tied to securing state water leases and making sure the buyer kept access to water, and could owe up to that amount back to Mahi Pono if those obligations weren't met. Plaintiffs in later litigation argued this directly: the sales agreement shows the land is worth \$62 million more with access to 30 mgd of permit-area water than without it. That's a market-revealed valuation of access, straight from an SEC filing, and it fits your "access, not ownership" framing well. sec[Environment-hawaii](https://www.environment-hawaii.org/?p=15726)

If you want a per-gallon comparison, you can calculate it yourself from the figures above: 31 mgd × 365 days, then divide the \$283,176 rent by that volume in thousands of gallons. Show that math in the paper so your instructor can follow it.
