You asked for feedback on the outline, in a folder you built for it, so this file is the first round.
East Maui water is a good subject for this paper — local, live, and with a real price already in it.
Two things are right before I get to what it needs. You budgeted the outline against the page limit,
section by section, which is the discipline most outlines skip and then pay for in the last hour. And
you wrote "once I verify exactly what each number represents" next to the $283,000 payment and the
$6.3 million estimate, instead of letting two numbers carry an argument they have not earned. Hold
onto that sentence; it is the paper's most important task.

**What each number is decides the paper.** If the $283,000 is what Mahi Pono pays for access under
the permit and the $6.3 million is a valuation of that access, then the gap between them is the rent
on a public resource, and your question — how should it be priced — has a number attached. If the
$6.3 million is something else — a valuation of the land, or of the crop — the gap means nothing.
Find the primary for each: the permit or lease record for the payment, and whoever produced the
estimate for the estimate, cited by name, with the date you retrieved it. Not news coverage of either.

**The outline has no recommendation, and Recommendation is the heaviest criterion.** It ends on
"Mahi Pono as an investment, and conclusion." Your own first section names the mechanism — a
permit and access arrangement and a price paid under it — so the question is which body sets the
price you would change: the Board of Land and Natural Resources on the permit terms, or the
Commission on Water Resource Management on the instream flow standards? Pick the decision-maker, and
the recommendation becomes a pricing or allocation rule they could adopt.

**Two graphs, one of them with a real number in it.** Graph 1, supply and demand for East Maui water:
supply is a stream flow, measurable in million gallons a day, so that axis can carry data. Demand is
the hard curve, and the two numbers above are the only price points you have. Graph 2, marginal
benefit against marginal cost: whose marginal benefit, in which use? Your own list — agricultural,
environmental, cultural, community — says the marginal analysis runs across uses. The marginal cost
of the last unit to agriculture is the marginal benefit that unit would have had in the stream. Make
that explicit and your two graphs are one argument; it also fixes the line under Graph 2, where the
opportunity cost is the value of the forgone use rather than Q* itself.

**"Mahi Pono as an investment" needs to earn its page.** If the section asks whether the operation
stays viable at a higher water price, that is the buyer's elasticity of demand and it belongs inside
the pricing argument. If it is about anything else, it is a different paper; cut it.

**In order:**

1. Verify what the $283,000 and the $6.3 million each represent, from primaries, with retrieval dates.
2. Name the decision-maker and draft the recommendation as a pricing or allocation rule.
3. Decide what each graph plots and which one carries measured data.
4. Fold the investment section into the pricing argument or cut it.

Your `feedback/README.md` says you will record what you changed in response, or why you did not. Put
that under this file.
