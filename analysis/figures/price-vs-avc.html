Price against average variable cost, one crop at a time

Each panel runs one crop by itself and asks the shutdown question: does the price of a bed cover what a bed costs to work, before any of the $20,000 season fixed cost is counted? Where the line sits below the price, every bed contributes something toward a fixed cost the farm owes whether it plants or not. Each panel has its own dollar scale — the crops are not meant to be compared by height, only each against its own price.

Average variable costThe quantity the plan actually plantsAVC above price — a bed that does not pay for itself

Average variable cost is every variable dollar that crop would spend at that acreage — labor for its hours, the farmer’s own at $34.72 until her 720 run out and hired at $17.36 after, plus fertilizer — divided by the beds planted. The $20,000 fixed cost is excluded by construction: it is owed either way, so it has no business in the planting decision. Each panel gives that crop the farmer’s full 720 hours, the same standalone convention the Marginal Analysis schedules use. Source: capabilities/marginal-analysis/model.xlsx, Marginal Analysis!B7:B87 and the Inputs rates.
