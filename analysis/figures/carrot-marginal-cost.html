Marginal cost and average variable cost of each carrot bed

Beds 1–20, built as though carrots were the only crop. The price a bed earns is fixed at $2,094. Marginal cost says where planting would stop on its own; average variable cost says whether planting is worth doing at all.

Labor at $34.72 — the farmer’s own hoursLabor at $17.36 — hired temporary hoursFertilizer, $440 a bedAverage variable cost

Standalone, the farmer’s 720 hours go to carrots alone and last until bed 17; in the farm’s mix they are used up by the tomatoes, so every carrot bed is worked at $17.36 and the twentieth costs $1,688.95. The dashed line is total variable cost (labor plus fertilizer) divided by beds planted. It never reaches the price, so every bed covers its own cost and leaves something toward the $20,000 season fixed cost — $3,511.08 across 20 beds. Marginal cost excludes that fixed cost.
